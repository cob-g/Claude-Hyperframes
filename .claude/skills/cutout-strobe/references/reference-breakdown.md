# Reference breakdown

The reference is a 19.3 s reel by the guitarist RJ Pasin for his track "remedy" (Facebook reel
1606171154238628, posted 2026-08-10): 579 frames at 30 fps, 16:9. He plays in a warehouse and on a
laser-lit stage; the edit cuts between those shots. Every number below was measured from the
file: frame by frame for the picture, and per 5 ms for the sound.

Nothing of the reel ships with this skill. The skill borrows its mechanics.

## What happens on screen

One performer is on screen for the whole film, and he is never strobed. In most shots he is cut
out and kept on top. Behind him, a window cut into the shot shows a montage of the film's other
shots, blurred until they are texture, changing on every frame. The window is a different shape
in every shot, and it moves in one direction while the shot lasts: a slit closes, blinds shut, an
X scissors down to a bar, a disc grows.

Between shots, the next shot arrives in pieces over the current one. First a white bar, then the
next shot inside the bar, then the next performer's silhouette in solid white, then the performer
in colour standing out of the bar. Two smeared frames later the new shot is the whole frame.

About once every two seconds the whole frame goes white for one frame and fades back over the
next thirteen.

There is no type, no logo, no caption, and no graphic other than white.

## Why it is so good

**1. One thing holds still.** The performer is the only sharp, steady thing in the frame, and
everything else changes 25 times a second. An eye with an anchor can take any speed around it.
That is why the film reads as fast and not as noise. Strobe the subject too and it becomes a
flicker with nobody in it.

**2. The picture is the waveform.** The track's bass plays in blocks with rests between them, and
the picture does the same thing on the same frames:

- Bass in: the frame is full. A cutout over a strobing montage.
- Bass out: the frame empties. The old shot holds and white shapes hit on the track's pulse.
- Bass back: two smeared frames, and the new shot is everywhere.
- The backbeat inside the block: one white frame.

The six bass blocks measure within 0.1 dB of each other, and each gets the same weight of picture.
With the sound off you can still see where the bass is.

**3. Every cut is announced.** No shot arrives whole. You see a bar of it, then more of it, then
its performer in white, then in colour, and only then the cut. Three to five hits in under half
a second count you in, so the cut lands as a release and not as a surprise. It happens every
1.92 s, and it is why the film never feels random.

**4. White is the only graphic.** Flash, bar, disc, column, silhouette: all pure white, all one
frame long. White is the percussion. Because the film is dark (mean luma 0.31) and nothing else is
ever white, a single white frame reads at full strength. A second colour would halve it.

**5. Each wall has one shape, and the shape goes somewhere.** Seven walls use six shapes: slit,
blinds, slit, X, corner panel, full frame, disc. Inside its wall each shape moves one way (closes,
shuts, grows), so a one-second shot has a start and an end, and the eye is led toward the flash.
The same trick with a new outline every two seconds looks like invention, not repetition.

**6. The montage is texture, not picture.** The shots behind the performer are spun or
zoom-blurred, pulled down to near grey, and darkened. They carry motion and rhythm and nothing
you need to read. Now and then one keeps its colour (the green of the laser stage, the orange of
a fretboard), which is enough to say that these are the film's own shots.

**7. A third of every cycle is calm.** After the flash come thirteen frames of a plain shot
under fading white, often a macro detail (a fretboard, a tattooed hand). Then the rest, where the
picture is the old shot plus a few white hits. So each 1.92 s is one second dense, half a second
calm, half a second geometric. Density with no rest would wear out in five seconds.

**8. Scale jumps at every cut.** Wide, medium, macro, medium, wide from below, medium, wide. The
performer is never the same size twice in a row, so each cut is felt even when the shots share a
place and a palette.

**9. The intro runs the other way.** For four seconds there is no bass, and the picture is the
most hidden it will ever be: the montage covers the whole frame and the real shot shows through
as the slit closes. The picture resolves while the sound builds. Then three stabs give three
white silhouettes, and the bass and the first wall land together.

**10. Two colours.** A steel-blue grade with crushed blacks, and orange where there is skin, wood,
or rust. The breakdown's run of shots pushes both harder, so the quietest part of the track is
the most colourful part of the picture.

## Sound design

The sound is one track, and the track was written with holes in it.

| What | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Loudness | -14.1 LUFS integrated, true peak -2.2 dBFS, loudness range 6.1 LU |
| Bass | 82% of the energy below 120 Hz; in for 50% of the film |
| Blocks | Six, each 1.43 to 1.77 s, each at -10.9 dBFS RMS (within 0.1 dB of each other) |
| Rests | 0.45 s each; the whole mix is 10 dB down in them and the bass 22 dB down |
| Intro | A tremolo build from -33.5 to -22.7 dBFS over 4 s, with no bass |
| Stabs | Three, 0.1 s each, with 0.07 s of silence between, just before the first block |
| Breakdown | 2.1 s at -20.9 dBFS in the middle; the film's run of plain shots sits on it |
| Stereo | L/R correlation 0.94 |
| Tempo | 93.75 BPM by onset comb; a 0.16 s pulse; a block starts every 1.92 s |

- **The rests are the edit points.** The bass does not fade into a rest. It stops inside two
  frames, and the picture breaks on the same frame (186, 244, 419, 474, 534 against bass-off
  measured on 187, 245, 418, 475, 533).
- **The stabs are drawn.** Each of the three stabs before the drop has a white silhouette on its
  first frame (128, 134, 139 against attacks on 129, 134, 139).
- **The flash is the backbeat.** Inside a block the guitar's attacks come every 2.4 frames and
  none is louder than the rest, so the flash cannot be found by loudness. It sits half a cycle
  after the bass comes in (29 frames), on the attack nearest that point.
- **The five-frame steps are on the pulse.** Columns on 345, 350, 355, 360 sit on attacks at
  344.9, 350.1, 354.9, 359.5. The three-frame steps are faster than the pulse and are not on it;
  they are short enough (four or five hits) that this does not show.
- **High end marks the rests.** The band above 2 kHz rises in every rest (a hiss or sweep under
  the stutter) and peaks on each flash. Whether that is part of the song or added in the edit
  cannot be told from the mix. The skill adds a tick on each step hit, a whoosh that peaks on each
  landing, and a hit on each flash, all well under the track.
- **No voice, no words.** Nothing competes with the picture for attention.

The lesson for choosing a track: it needs a bass that plays in blocks and stops dead. A track
whose bass never rests gives the edit nowhere to step. `bass-map.mjs` measures this.
